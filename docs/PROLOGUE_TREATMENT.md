## The Framing Device (recommended)

**Make the poem diegetic.**
It isn't narration from nowhere — it's a real text in the world.
Every child of every race knows it.
It's sung at festivals, carved over shrine doors, taught before letters.
That buys you three things for free:

1. **The skip button is justified in-fiction.**
   Second character onward, the prompt reads _"You know this song."_ — not a UI concession, a character beat.
2. **A collectible.**
   The version the prologue recites is the _short_ version.
   Later, in the world, the player finds the full text — including the last stanzas, the ones nobody sings anymore, the ones that describe the second turning.
   Someone wrote them _after_.
   Someone saw it coming.
   That's a lorebook unlock and a story hook in one.
3. **A tonal license.**
   A children's rhyme can be beautiful and simple and still be completely true.
   The horror lands harder when the warning was in a nursery song.

**On the Rothfuss register:** his signature isn't ornate vocabulary, it's _rhythm and triads_ — three-part sentences, three-part silences, plain words placed with weight.
Write the connecting prose in threes and let the poem carry the ornament.
Short sentences.
Concrete nouns.
Never explain the thing you just made the reader feel.

## Structure

14 panels.
Each panel = **1–2 stanzas of the poem, then 1–3 short prose paragraphs.**
Poem carries the myth; prose carries the meaning.
Alternate, never blend.

At ~12–18 seconds a panel (with an advance-on-click and a hold), that's roughly **3–4 minutes**.
Right at the ceiling for an unskippable-first-time prologue.

Panels marked ⭐ are the load-bearing ones.
If you have to cut for pacing, cut elsewhere.

## Panel Breakdown

### I — The Silence

**Poem:** stanza 1 (_"In the beginning, there was naught but a voice…"_)

**Prose beat:** Establish absence.
Not darkness, not cold — _absence_.
There was no world to be dark.
Three sentences, all negation, then one that isn't.

> _Draft:_ There was no world yet, and so there was no night.
> No cold, because there was nothing to be cold.
> No silence either, truly — silence is a thing a world does when it stops, and nothing had started.
> Then something spoke.

---

### II — The Waking

**Poem:** stanzas 2–3 (_"At first, they seemed insignificant…"_ / _"These ethereal beings, devoid of any form…"_)

**Prose beat:** These were us.
Before us.
Say it plainly and move on — don't dwell.

---

### III — The Dreaming

**Poem:** stanzas 4–5 (_"They learned to think…"_ / _"In this kingdom, they built wonders unknown…"_)

**Prose beat:** They could imagine anything and touch nothing.
Their cities were perfect because nothing real had to hold them up.
Seed the craving.

---

### IV — The Gift ⭐

**Poem:** stanzas 6–7, compressed

**Prose beat:** **Name the three layers here.**
Thought imagines.
Magic carries.
Matter holds.
This is the single most important idea in the game's cosmology and it should be stated once, cleanly, in a triad, and then never over-explained.

> _Draft:_ Thought is the first layer, and it can imagine anything.
> Matter is the third layer, and it can hold anything.
> Magic is the second, and it is the only reason the first can ever reach the third.

---

### V — The World Made

**Poem:** stanza 10 — **first appearance of the refrain** (_"For thoughts, magic, and the physical plane…"_), then 11

**Prose beat:** Short.
Let the image do it.
One sentence of prose, maybe none.

---

### VI — The Choosing ⭐

**Poem:** stanzas 11–13 (_"…adopting bodies, materializing into the light. / Each form unique, from ephemeral blur to mortal man"_)

**Prose beat:** This is the fork, and your poem already contains it — _"from ephemeral blur to mortal man."_
**Some thoughts took bodies.**
**Some did not.**
The ones who took form gained the world and lost forever: bodies end, and when they end the thought inside them scatters.
The ones who stayed as light kept forever and lost the world: they can want, and remember, and never touch.

Neither choice was wrong.
Both were incomplete.
That's what makes the next panel inevitable rather than convenient.

**Note on the races:** don't say _"and there were three."_
The account-milestone system unlocks additional races later.
Say **"three learned the bond first"** or **"three still keep it"** — same weight, leaves the door open.

**Note on the beasts:** stanza 13 (dragons, wolves) is doing more work than it may appear.
If animals are also descended thoughts — dimmer, simpler, still _thought_ — then the Hollowing has something in a bear to hollow.
The Feral Beasts stop being a generic tutorial mob and become the mundane, deniable edge of the same tragedy.
Recommend keeping that stanza for exactly this reason.

---

### VII — The Bond ⭐ _(TITLE CARD)_

**Poem:** new stanza required — written since, as Panel VII of `PROLOGUE.md`.

**Prose beat:** The covenant, in one exchange.
_The wisp holds your thoughts._
_You lend it your hands._
When the body ends, the wisp keeps what the body was carrying — and gives it back to whatever comes next.
Nothing that matters has to be lost.

This is where the card system becomes _mythology instead of UI._
Cards are the soul because thought was the first layer.
The spirit holds them because holding is the only thing a formless thing can still do.
Say it once, here, warmly, and the player never needs a tutorial popup explaining why cards survive death.

**Title card — "SPIRITBOUND" — lands over this image.**
The word is literally what just happened.

---

### VIII — The Law

**Poem:** stanzas 14, 16 (the Voice laughs; _"balance was the key"_)

**Prose beat:** The one law, phrased so it can be broken later in two different ways.
Something like: _take only what you can give back._
Not a commandment — a description of how the three layers stay stacked.

---

### IX — The First Breaking

**Poem:** stanzas 17–19

**Prose beat:** They took more magic than they returned.
Keep it beautiful — white towers, blinding light — not grim.
The world the game inherits is kind; its catastrophes should look like glory going wrong.

---

### X — The Silence Returns

**Poem:** stanzas 20, 22

**Prose beat:** **This is where you use the triad.**
The silence has parts.
The Voice stopped.
The magic stopped answering.
And then the people stopped asking.
Three stages, three sentences, in that order.

---

### XI — The Mending

**Poem:** stanzas 23–24

**Prose beat:** They forgave each other and rebuilt.
Crucially: **the wisps stayed.**
The Voice left; the bond didn't.
Everything the world has now, it has because the formless ones kept their side of a bargain nobody was left to enforce.

---

### XII — The Long Peace ⭐ _(tone anchor)_

**Poem:** the refrain, full (stanza 26)

**Prose beat:** Bright, uncomplicated, generous.
Spirit-warriors honored.
Shrines sunlit.
Festivals.
**This panel must be the warmest image in the entire sequence** — it's the promise the rest of the game breaks.
If the player doesn't love this frame, nothing later hurts.

Hold it a beat longer than the others.

---

### XIII — The Second Turning ⭐

**Poem:** new stanza — one of "the ones nobody sings."
Written since, as Panel XIII of `PROLOGUE.md`.

**Prose beat:** The mistake, repeated — **but inverted, and say so.**
The first time, the ones with bodies took more magic than they could give back.
This time it's the ones without.
And what a formless thing hungers for isn't magic.
It's the thing it was trusted to hold.

That inversion is the whole cosmology paying off in two sentences.
The Ascendant, the Hollowing, the devouring — all of it is _the same sin from the other side of the bond._

Do **not** explain further.
No factions named.
No conspiracy revealed.
One wisp, turning, over a sleeping village.

---

### XIV — You

**Poem:** new closing couplet.
Turn to second person on the last line.
The song has been about _them_ for thirteen panels; it becomes about _you_ in one.

**Prose beat:** Four sentences maximum, present tense, second person.
Your spirit purged your cards and threw you from the cliff.
You don't know why.
**Nobody watching this scene should know why either.**

⚠️ **Art direction, canon-critical:** the spirit in this image reads as **grieving, not enraged.**
Anguish, not malice.
Per `LORE.md` the betrayal/rescue ambiguity must survive the entire first act — if the prologue image shows a monster, the question is answered in the first four minutes and the best long arc is dead.

**Hard cut** to black.
Then gameplay: you wake at the bottom with nothing.

## Poem Revision — Recommendations

> Executed.
> See `PROLOGUE.md` and its "Notes on the Revision" section.
> Kept here because the stanza numbers below are the reasoning behind that revision.

**1 — Compress the "magic arrived and was good" block.**
Stanzas 6, 8, 9 and 10 are four consecutive stanzas making one point.
Merge to two.
This is the only place the poem loses momentum, and it's right before the best material.

Same treatment, lighter, on the mending section (23–25): three stanzas of renewal become two.

That takes 26 stanzas to roughly 20, which is the right length for 14 panels at 1–2 each.

**2 — Add three stanzas.**
The poem currently ends at the mending — but the story starts after that.
Needed:

- **The Bond** (Panel VII) — the covenant between the formless and the formed.
- **The Second Turning** (Panel XIII) — the mistake repeating, inverted.
- **The Turn to You** (Panel XIV) — a closing couplet in second person.

Frame the last two as _the stanzas nobody sings anymore_ and they double as the collectible lorebook payoff.

**3 — Meter, lightly.**
The poem runs mostly anapestic-ish tetrameter but drifts.
It doesn't need to be metronomic — a recited song shouldn't be — but a few lines fight the tongue on a read-aloud.
Fix only those.
Reading it out loud will find them faster than scanning it will.

Sample of the direction, on stanza 3:

> _Original:_
> These ethereal beings, devoid of any form,
> Freed from the shackles of any known norm,
> Ventured into the vastness of the cosmic unknown,
> And danced amidst stars, in the cosmos, alone.

> _Tightened:_
> These weightless beings, wanting of form,
> free of all law, unbound by norm,
> ventured out into the vast unknown
> and danced amidst the stars, alone.

Same content, same rhymes, less friction, and "wanting of form" now does double duty — lacking it _and_ longing for it — which sets up Panel VI four stanzas early.

## The Effect

It's called **parallax** (the film term is the **multiplane camera**).
Combined with a slow push-in, that's the **Ken Burns effect**.
In a 2D engine it's trivial and it looks far more expensive than it is.

> Production notes previously in this section are superseded by `PROLOGUE_SHOTLIST.md`, which carries the overscan, plane-cutting, consistency and style-block guidance in more detail.
> One point is worth repeating because it is easy to undo by accident: **do not let the model paint the wisps.**
> Painted wisps are static wisps.
> Spawn them as a Phaser particle emitter over the top.

## Canon Flags — for LORE.md

This treatment creates canon that is not in `LORE.md` yet.
Worth writing up before it drifts.

| New canon                                   | Note                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The three layers** (thought/magic/matter) | The cosmology's spine. Currently only in the poem.                                                                                            |
| **Wisps = thoughts that never took form**   | Directly explains why spirits hold cards: cards are thought, thought is the first layer.                                                      |
| **The bond as covenant**                    | The wisp holds; the mortal acts. Grounds the entire spirit system.                                                                            |
| **Races: human, dwarf, elf**                | Phrase as "three that kept the bond first" — account milestones unlock more.                                                                  |
| **Beasts are dim thoughts too**             | Makes Feral Beasts part of the cosmology rather than filler. Recommended.                                                                     |
| **Two falls, inverted**                     | First: the formed hoarded magic. Second: the formless hoard souls. The thematic keystone — the Ascendant become a repetition, not a new idea. |
| **The Voice's silence — unresolved**        | Deliberately leave open whether it returns, or whether it ever left. Endgame material. Do not answer it in the prologue.                      |
