# Spiritbound — World & Lore (canon)

This is the canonical lore source (owned by the `lore-keeper` skill). New canon is
not real until it is written here. Names marked *(provisional)* are open to change —
keep them consistent once chosen.

## Tone & Aesthetic

High fantasy, **bright, friendly, welcoming** on the surface — sunny villages,
grateful townsfolk, festivals honoring spirit-warriors, sunlit shrines that hum
with warmth. The horror lives in the *cracks*, not the lighting. The signature
contrast: a world that looks like a fairy tale while a quiet conspiracy rots it from
within. Think welcoming-but-uncanny. Do not make the world grimdark; make it
*kind*, so that the wrongness, when it shows, lands harder.

## The Spirit Cosmology

- People bind to **spirits**, who hold, grant, and (rarely) purge a person's
  **cards** — and cards are the soul. This is lived reality, treated as ordinary,
  not as game UI.
- A true spirit's purpose is to **steward** souls: hold them safe, return them,
  protect their bearer. Spirit-warriors — called **the Spiritbound** *(provisional)*
  — are revered by common folk for exactly this: they help and they protect.
- **Corruption = devouring instead of stewarding.** A spirit can gain power by
  *consuming* the souls/cards it was meant to guard. That hunger blackens it (dark
  hue, hateful features) and its rot leaks into the surrounding land. This single
  mechanism is the root of every enemy in the world.
- The leak is called **the Hollowing** *(provisional)*. Where it spreads, animals
  turn feral and people can be emptied of their souls.

## The Conspiracy (the central truth)

Most Spiritbound are sincere. But a hidden faction within the order — **the
Ascendant** *(provisional)* — believes spirits should *rule* over the people they
were meant to serve. Their spirits devour to grow strong enough to do it. From the
outside, the Ascendant are indistinguishable from true Spiritbound: same honors,
same shrines, same public love. **That is the source of the game's paranoia — you
cannot tell friend from foe by looking.**

- The order's public ideal: tend and shield.
- The Ascendant's hidden creed: the strong spirit should command, not protect.
- An unknown few — the hero among them — have glimpsed the truth and trust no one.
  A nascent underground of the disillusioned may exist (story hook).

## The Hero's Situation

The hero discovered the truth, and his spirit abandoned him — purging his cards and
throwing him off a cliff (see Opening in GAME_DESIGN.md). 

**Central ambiguity (do not resolve early):** was that betrayal, or rescue? A spirit
purging and discarding a warrior reads publicly as rejection. But a *true* spirit,
seeing its bearer marked for devouring, might violently purge-and-cast-out to keep
those cards from being consumed — sacrificing the bond to save the soul. The hero
does not know which it was. Neither should the player, for a long time. This is the
seed of the eventual reconciliation questline (win back the original purged cards).

## Enemy Taxonomy

Every tier is a symptom of the same rot (devouring spirits), escalating from mundane
→ tragic → alien → social → apex. Avoid generic fantasy monsters that don't connect
to the cosmology.

1. **Feral Beasts** *(mundane)* — natural animals turned aggressive by Hollowing
   leaking from tainted spirit-sites. Early-game, grounded (e.g. a bear rampaging a
   village). They are *victims*, and the world blames bad luck — deniable symptoms
   that only the hero reads correctly.

2. **The Hollowed** *(tragic)* — people, often former Spiritbound, whose cards/souls
   were devoured, leaving an empty body driven by residual stolen spirit-light. They
   mirror the hero's near-fate. Played for pity, not menace.

3. **Sendings** *(alien)* — constructs woven from stolen cards + raw spirit-essence,
   conjured by the Ascendant to hunt the hero specifically. Not alive, not natural:
   wrong geometry, glowing seams. **Each embodies a stolen ability** (a Sending made
   from a flame-card breathes fire — ties enemies to the card system). **Scales with
   the hero's notoriety**: the more truth uncovered, the more (and deadlier) the
   hunt — a dynamic threat, not a static spawn.

4. **The Turned** *(social)* — Spiritbound who joined the Ascendant, still revered,
   still walking among normal folk. Indistinguishable from true heroes from outside.
   Elites/minibosses; the engine of "trust no one" — some friendly quest-givers are
   Turned.

5. **Mad Spirits** *(bosses)* — spirits that devoured too much: immense, dark,
   hateful. **They fight using the cards they stole** from heroes they hollowed, so
   players face weaponized versions of abilities they recognize. Some are *fallen
   beloved location-spirits* — a hub spirit the player trusted early can fall later,
   becoming a boss. Apex encounters.

## Story Hooks (open threads)

- **Reconciliation with the hero's own spirit** — and resolving the betrayal/rescue
  ambiguity. Likely an endgame arc.
- **The notoriety/hunt system** — Sendings intensify as the player learns more,
  mechanizing the conspiracy "noticing" you.
- **A fallen hub spirit** — set up a beloved early location-spirit to later turn Mad,
  for maximum betrayal.
- **The underground** — do the disillusioned organize? A counter-faction the player
  might build or join.

## Naming Conventions

Keep names warm and high-fantasy on the surface, with weight underneath. Established
/ provisional terms (scan before coining new ones):
- **the Spiritbound** — spirit-warriors (also the working game title). *(provisional)*
- **the Ascendant** — the hidden ruling-spirit conspiracy. *(provisional)*
- **the Hollowing** — the corruption/rot that leaks from devouring spirits. *(provisional)*
- **the Hollowed** — people emptied of their souls. *(provisional)*
- **Sendings** — conjured spirit-essence constructs sent to hunt. *(provisional)*
- **Mad Spirits** — corrupted spirits (boss tier). *(provisional)*
- True spirits *steward*; corrupt spirits *devour*; warriors who fall are *Turned*.
