# Prologue Shot List

**Not one image per chapter — one image per _subject_.**
A stanza that stays on the same thing shares a plate; a stanza that moves gets a new
one.
That comes to **23 unique generations plus 3 cheap variants**, and it fixes the biggest
risk in a sequence like this, which is not quality — it's fourteen wide shots in a row
and a player's eye going flat by panel six.

Two structural rules do most of the work:

**1 — Vary the shot size deliberately.**
Cosmic wide → mid → intimate close, and back.
The column is in the table.
The sequence should physically zoom in as it goes: it starts at the scale of a universe
and ends at the scale of two hands.

**2 — Four motifs recur, and three of them invert.**
This is the spine.
Each is the same composition shown twice, warm then wrong — so the second one hurts
without a word of explanation.

| Motif                | First | Again                  | What the repeat does                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------- | ----- | ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A · The Mote**     | I.1   | VII.1 → XIII.1 → XIV.1 | The spark the Voice struck is the same light that opens its hands on you. Literally the same rendered asset, retinted.                                                                              |
| **B · Cupped Hands** | VII.1 | XIV.2                  | Hands closed around a light / hands open and empty. Opening your hands is dropping something _or_ letting it go. The image is the ambiguity.                                                        |
| **C · Three Panes**  | V.R   | VIII.R → XII.R → XIV.R | The refrain gets a recurring plate, not a new image. One base, three repaints. Fourth time a pane is cracked.                                                                                       |
| **D · The Keeping**  | XI.3  | XIII.2                 | A wisp drawing a light out of a dying person's chest and _holding_ it — then the identical shot where it closes around the light instead. Teaches the death mechanic and the villain in two frames. |

## The Shots

Append the style block (bottom of file) to every prompt.
Shot sizes: **XW** extreme wide · **W** wide · **M** medium · **CU** close-up.

### I — The Silence

**I.1 · XW · ⭐ MOTIF A**
`an infinite black void filled with faint distant stars, a single warm point of golden light suspended alone in the emptiness, nothing else, vast negative space, immense scale`
→ Save this mote as a separate transparent asset.
It is reused four times.

### II — The Waking

**II.1 · XW**
`countless tiny motes of pale light drifting through a deep starfield, faint and formless, some clustered together like a shoal of fish, cosmic scale, empty dark space`

**II.2 · W · optional**
`streams of small lights flowing between enormous distant stars, leaving faint trails, dancing in a spiral, dark cosmic emptiness, lonely and beautiful`
→ Cuttable.
II.1 with good parallax may carry both stanzas.

### III — The Dreaming

**III.1 · W**
`translucent ghostly cities and impossible spires made of pure light, half-real and unfinished, floating in a starless void, no ground beneath them, dreamlike architecture`

**III.2 · M · first close beat**
`a single small light hovering at the edge of a translucent dream-creature with luminous wings, reaching toward it, passing through it, unable to touch, longing, dark void background`
→ This images _"nothing they made could be held"_.
First time the sequence gets small and sad.
Don't cut it — it's what makes the gift in IV feel earned.

### IV — The Gift

**IV.1 · XW · vertical composition**
`a great silent presence of radiant light high above, threads of shimmering magic descending like rain onto drifting motes of light below, the motes brightening where the threads touch, cosmic awe, gold and violet`

### V — The World Made

**V.R · W · ⭐ MOTIF C — REFRAIN PLATE (base)**
`three vast transparent layers stacked like panes of glass, a starfield above, a shimmering veil of light between, a green living world below, perfectly aligned, symmetrical, sacred geometry, luminous, dark background`
→ Generate this once, well.
Everything after is inpaint variants.
Each pane must be a separate parallax plane — the parallax _is_ the idea.

**V.1 · M**
`figures forming out of streams of golden light and wet clay on a hillside at dawn, bodies half light and half flesh, taking shape, warm morning glow, wonder`

### VI — The Choosing

**VI.1 · W**
`great golden dragons and running wolves and strange beasts forming out of streams of light across a wide landscape at dawn, many different shapes emerging at once, epic, painterly`

**VI.2 · M · ⭐ KEYSTONE — split composition**
`on the left three figures standing on grass casting long shadows, an elf a dwarf and a human, solid and real, on the right formless lights hovering above the same grass casting no shadow at all, dawn light, quiet, symbolic`
→ **The whole panel is legible or it isn't, in one glance: the bodied touch the ground,
the lights don't.**
If the shadows don't read, regenerate.
Nothing else in this shot matters.

### VII — The Bond

**VII.1 · CU · ⭐ MOTIF A + B · TITLE CARD**
`close up of two cupped hands holding a small glowing wisp of light, warm gold light spilling between the fingers, dark background, illuminating a face above from below, gentle, intimate, sacred`
→ Composition note: frame this so the **empty version** works.
XIV.2 is this shot with the light gone.
Same crop, same hands, same angle.

### VIII — The Law

**VIII.1 · XW**
`stars and green forests and great beasts and dark oceans bursting outward from a single point of light, creation expanding, joyous, immense, gold and deep blue`

**VIII.R · W · MOTIF C variant 1**
Inpaint the base plate: add a single thin line of light running cleanly through all
three panes.
Nothing else changes.

### IX — The First Breaking

**IX.1 · M**
`robed figures pulling streams of glowing magic into themselves, hoarding the light, other figures around them going dim and grey, greed, beautiful and wrong, ancient white architecture`
→ Show the _crime_ before the _war_.
The taking is the sin; the burning is only the consequence.

**IX.2 · W**
`a magnificent ancient city at war, towers of white stone cracking and burning, torrents of wild magic tearing through the streets, figures wielding blinding light against one another, beautiful and terrible`

### X — The Silence Returns

**X.1 · W**
`the ruins of a great city under a dead grey sky, thick dust, broken statues half buried in rubble, no light anywhere, desolate, muted palette`

**X.2 · low angle · ⭐ near-empty frame**
`a single small figure kneeling at the very bottom of the frame among grey rubble, above them nothing but vast empty overcast grey sky filling most of the image, no light, no birds, utter emptiness`
→ The emotional floor.
**Kill the parallax entirely on this one** — one plane, no motion, hold it two beats
longer than anything else.
Stillness is the effect.

### XI — The Mending

**XI.1 · W**
`people of three kinds rebuilding a village together at sunrise, green shoots pushing through cracked stone, hopeful, warm golden morning light, painterly`

**XI.2 · M · the song being made**
`an elder telling a story to children around a small fire at night, the children listening, simple village, warm firelight, intimate, storybook`
→ Diegetic payoff: the player is watching the poem they're currently hearing get
written.

**XI.3 · CU · ⭐ MOTIF D**
`a gentle wisp of light hovering above the chest of a peacefully dying old man in a bed, drawing a soft small light up out of him and cradling it, warm, tender, sorrowful but kind, dim room`
→ **This teaches the death-and-retention mechanic with no tutorial text.**
Frame it so the identical shot works with the emotion reversed.
XIII.2 is this shot, wrong.

### XII — The Long Peace

**XII.1 · W · ⭐ WARMEST FRAME IN THE SEQUENCE**
`a bright sunlit fantasy village square during a festival, colorful banners and flower garlands, humans dwarves and elves celebrating together, glowing wisps drifting above a flower covered shrine, joyful, warm, welcoming, storybook illustration`
→ Most motion of any panel.
This is the promise the game breaks; if it isn't lovely, nothing later lands.

**XII.R · W · MOTIF C variant 2**
Inpaint the base plate warm — sunlight through the panes, the world-pane green and
full.

### XIII — The Second Turning

**XIII.1 · M · ⭐ MOTIF A**
`a single wisp of light with darkness creeping in at its edges, hovering over a sleeping village at dusk, the grass directly beneath it greying and curling, subtle wrongness, still beautiful, muted blue evening light`

**XIII.2 · CU · ⭐ MOTIF D — inverted**
`a wisp of light closing around a small glowing soul it has drawn from the chest of a young woman who is awake and afraid, the wisp dark at its edges, the light being pulled in and not released, dim room, quiet horror`
→ Same crop and angle as XI.3.
Held / kept.
That pair is the entire cosmology in two frames.

### XIV — You

**XIV.R · W · MOTIF C variant 3**
Inpaint the base plate: one pane cracked, the alignment slipped, the line of light from
VIII.R broken.

**XIV.1 · W · ⭐ MOTIF A**
`a lone warrior at the edge of a high cliff at dusk, a large luminous spirit looming over him with an anguished sorrowful presence, glowing cards torn from his chest and scattering on the wind, dramatic backlight, tragic, painterly`
→ ⚠️ **Grief, not rage.**
If the spirit reads as a monster the betrayal-or-rescue question is answered in minute
four and the arc is dead.
If a generation looks menacing, discard it however good it is.

**XIV.2 · CU · ⭐ MOTIF B — inverted · final frame**
`close up of two empty open cupped hands in darkness, no light in them, the last faint glow fading from the fingers, cold blue, loss, emptiness`
→ Hard cut to black.
Then gameplay.

## Getting 23 Consistent Images Out of Fooocus

The hard part is not any single image — it's that image 19 still looks like image 3.

**1 — Generate the palette anchors first**, in this order: **XII.1** (warmest), **X.2**
(coldest), **VII.1** (intimate).
Those three define the range.
Everything else has to live between them, so make them good before making anything
else.

**2 — Then lock consistency with Image Prompt.**
Take the best anchor, load it under Input Image → **Image Prompt** in `ImagePrompt`
mode at weight ~0.4–0.6, and leave it there for every remaining generation.
This is the actual consistency lever in Fooocus.
A fixed seed will _not_ do it — seeds only hold across identical prompts.

**3 — Lock one checkpoint and one style set** and don't touch them mid-sequence.
Fooocus V2 + Fooocus Masterpiece + a fantasy-art style is a reasonable start.
Changing the base model halfway will show.

**4 — Generate in blocks**, not in story order: cosmic (I–IV, VIII.1), world (V–VI, IX,
XI), intimate (VII.1, XI.3, XIII.2, XIV.2), turn (XIII.1, XIV.1).
Drift within a block matters much less than drift between neighbouring shots.

**5 — Outpaint every keeper** ~20% left and right at 1344×768 before cutting planes.
Pan across a viewport-sized plate and the edge shows within a second.

**6 — Then cut 3 planes and inpaint the holes** — far / mid / near, filling what the
moving foreground reveals.

**Style block — append to every prompt:**

> `ethereal high fantasy illustration, painterly oil texture, luminous, warm gold and deep indigo palette, soft volumetric light, storybook, wide cinematic composition`

**Negative — every prompt:**

> `text, watermark, signature, frame, border, photorealistic, 3d render, glowing bokeh particles, modern clothing, cluttered composition`

The `glowing bokeh particles` exclusion is deliberate and load-bearing: **do not let the
model paint the wisps.**
Painted wisps are static wisps, and they appear in nine of these shots.
Spawn them as a Phaser particle emitter over the top — one emitter, reused everywhere,
and it's what will make the whole thing read as a cutscene instead of a slideshow.

## If You Have to Cut

Trim in this order: **II.2**, then **VI.1**, then **XI.2**, then **IX.1**.

Never cut **VI.2** (the fork), **VII.1 / XIV.2** (the hands), **XI.3 / XIII.2** (the
keeping), or **X.2** (just sky).
Four of those five are halves of a pair — cutting one costs both.
