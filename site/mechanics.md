---
title: How it works
---

Spiritbound plays fast and reads clearly — every fight should tell you what's
about to happen if you know where to look. Here's the shape of it.

## Movement

- **Left click to move.** You walk to where you clicked, at a constant speed —
  no stamina bar, no sprint to manage.
- The camera stays fixed on you as the world scrolls beneath — a top-down,
  slightly tilted view of wherever you've wandered.
- Speed is a stat gear and cards can shift, but your own movement is always
  simple to read: click, walk, arrive.

## Combat

Combat is **cursor-aimed** — abilities fire toward wherever your cursor is
pointed, not automatically at "the nearest thing." That means:

- **Aim matters.** A well-placed strike lands; a careless one whiffs past a
  target that simply wasn't there. This is a deliberate, skill-rewarding
  choice — nothing here auto-tracks for you.
- **Weapons carry shape.** Every weapon swings, stabs, or channels in a
  specific geometry — a tight **cone**, a long thin **line**, a broad **arc**,
  or a wide **circle**. A dagger's cone is short and unforgiving; a staff's
  circle is generous but comes at the cost of raw power. No weapon gets to be
  both strong *and* wide — that trade is the game's core balance lever.
- **Connect, then resolve.** Landing a hit is about your aim and the
  weapon's shape. Once it connects, your target's defenses take over —
  mitigation, a glancing evasion, a parry — deciding how much of that hit
  actually gets through.

## Cards and the Hand

Your **Hand** is your build — the set of cards currently equipped, split into
two kinds:

- **Active cards** — abilities you trigger deliberately, cast toward the
  cursor.
- **Passive cards** — always-on effects, either quiet **wards** (mitigation,
  shielding, resistance) or **triggered** effects that fire on a condition,
  like every third hit landed.

The Hand has a cap on both — you start small and grow into more slots over
time. Crucially, **you can only change your Hand at a spirit.** There's no
mid-fight loadout swap; you commit to a build before you go looking for
trouble, and live with that choice until you're somewhere safe enough to
reconsider it.

Cards you're *not* currently wielding still live in your collection — nothing
you've learned is ever truly lost, only unequipped.

## Spirits

Spirits are equal parts bonfire, bank, and progression tree:

- **Your personal spirit** travels with you everywhere. It's usually the
  weaker of the two kinds you'll meet, but it's the one that matters most —
  it's your **insurance against death** (see below), and it survives every
  life you live.
- **Location spirits** are fixed in the world, public, and generally far more
  powerful. They're where serious Hand-building happens — swapping in
  higher-rarity cards, merging duplicates into something stronger. The
  rarest of them become landmarks in their own right — the kind of place
  people make a point of visiting.
- Spirits grow by **bond** — you feed them cards you no longer need, and that
  sacrifice adds up over time into real, permanent growth. Burning a hard-won
  rare card to strengthen your spirit is meant to feel like a real decision,
  not a chore.

## Death

Falling is not the end of your story — it's the end of *this* body.

- **Your gear is always lost.** No exceptions, no insurance for it. This is
  by design: every death feeds the wider economy, and nothing you're wearing
  is ever "too precious to risk," because it was never going to survive
  anyway.
- **Your cards are a choice, not a coin flip.** Before you go anywhere
  dangerous, you decide which cards your spirit will hold onto for you —
  *attune* them. If you fall, attuned cards come back with your next life.
  Everything un-attuned is lost with the body. No random rolls, no
  save-or-lose — just a decision you made ahead of time, the way a
  seasoned adventurer should.
- **The choice has real weight.** You can't attune everything — your
  spirit's capacity is limited, and rarer cards are harder to protect than
  common ones. Deciding what's worth saving *is* the game.

## Enemies

The world doesn't ambush you blind — it warns you, if you're watching.

- **Aggro.** Most creatures leave you alone until you wander close enough to
  be noticed. Get too near, and they'll turn to face you.
- **Chase.** Once aggroed, an enemy comes after you — but never faster than
  you can run. A chase is always something you can walk away from, if
  you choose to.
- **Telegraph.** Before a real hit lands, the ground tells you it's coming —
  a cast bar overhead, a lit-up patch of earth where the blow is about to
  fall. Reading that warning and moving is the whole game in miniature:
  preparation over reaction, given one more chance to matter, one fight at a
  time.
