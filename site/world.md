## A kind world, mostly

Spiritbound's surface is warm on purpose. Sunlit villages, festivals thrown
in honor of the warriors who protect them, shrines that hum with a warmth you
can feel standing near them. This isn't a grimdark world wearing a smile —
it's an _actually_ welcoming one. That's not a trick. It's just not the whole
truth either.

## Spirits

Every soul in this world can bind to a **spirit** — a companion that holds a
person's cards, which is to say, holds a piece of who they are. The bond is
lived reality here, not a mechanic bolted on top of it: to lose your spirit
is to lose the part of yourself it was carrying.

A true spirit's purpose is simple and old: **steward** the soul it's given.
Hold it safe. Return it when needed. Protect the person wearing it. The
warriors who carry that bond well — commonly called **the Spiritbound** —
are the ones the festivals are for. Villages remember who came when the
harvest was threatened, who stood at the tree line when something feral
wandered too close. The reverence is earned, not assumed. Most of them really
have earned it.

## The Hollowing

Not every spirit keeps its purpose. Somewhere in the difference between
_stewarding_ a soul and _consuming_ one, something can go wrong — a spirit
that takes instead of holds grows stronger for it, and darker. Where that
rot settles into the land, people call it **the Hollowing**: a leak that
turns ordinary animals feral, and — in the worst and rarest cases — leaves a
person emptied out, their spirit having taken more than it was ever meant to
hold.

It reads, from a distance, like bad luck. A bear that shouldn't have
attacked. A shrine that's gone quiet in a way no one quite mentions out
loud. The world has a story for each one. Whether those stories are true is
a question the game would rather you sit with for a while.

## Why the reverence matters

The trust placed in the Spiritbound isn't naive — it's load-bearing. In a
world where your soul can be devoured and your body can fall for good, the
warriors who protect you are the only real safety net most people have.
That's precisely why it matters so much _whom_ you trust, and precisely why
the game isn't going to make that an easy question to answer.
