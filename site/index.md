Somewhere past the last friendly milestone, past the shrines that still hum warm
at dusk, the road opens onto **Spiritbound** — a world of sunlit villages,
grateful townsfolk, and festivals thrown in honor of the warriors who guard them.

It is, on the surface, a *kind* world. Keep that in mind. It matters later.

## Your soul rides with a spirit

In Spiritbound, every warrior binds to a **spirit** — a companion that holds
their **cards**. Not metaphorically. Cards *are* the soul here: your active
strikes, your wards, the quiet magic that keeps you standing. A true spirit's
whole purpose is to steward that soul — to hold it safe, to return it, to
protect the one who carries it.

That's the core split the game is built on:

- **Cards are your soul.** Learned, merged, evolved, carried between lives —
  a true spirit can preserve what matters most, even past death.
- **Gear is your body.** Armor, weapons, trinkets — always physical, always
  lost when you fall. Nothing to mourn twice; a fresh body is easy to find,
  a soul is not.

## Death is almost permanent

This is not a world where dying costs you a respawn timer. When your body
falls, it *stays* fallen — a new life begins. What survives is only what your
spirit chose to hold onto: the cards you trusted it with, attuned and set
aside before you ever set out. Everything else — every ring, every blade,
every potion in your pack — is gone with the body that carried it.

That's why preparation matters more than reaction. You don't gear up *during*
a fight for your life; you decide, at a spirit, what's worth saving *before*
you go looking for one.

## Your hand is your build

There are no classes here — no locked trees, no forced roles. What you *are*
emerges from what you carry: which cards you've learned, which weapon you've
chosen to wield, which stats your gear leans toward. A dagger rewards precise,
aggressive aim. A staff forgives a wider swing but trades away its bite. Every
card responds to what's feeding it — so the same card can feel like a
different weapon in different hands.

And because aim is yours to place — abilities fire toward the cursor, not
just "at" a target — landing a hit is a skill in itself, not a dice roll.
Missing is possible. Missing is *yours*.

## What lives in the cracks

The villages are warm. The shrines really do hum. The Spiritbound really are
loved, and mostly, they've earned it.

But not every spirit stewards what it's given. Somewhere out past the fields
you'll find places where the warmth curdles — animals that shouldn't be
aggressive, sites that feel *wrong* in a way no one else seems to notice.
The world won't tell you why. That's yours to find out.
